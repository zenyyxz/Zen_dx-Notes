## 1. Data vs. Information

| Parameter      | Data                                                        | Information                                                                       |
| :------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition** | Raw, unorganized facts, figures, symbols, or observations.  | Data that has been processed, structured, and contextualized to have meaning.     |
| **Context**    | Lacks context; meaningless on its own (e.g., `85, 92, 78`). | Set within a meaningful context (e.g., "Student's marks in ICT, Maths, Physics"). |
| **Dependence** | Independent of information.                                 | Directly depends on data and processing.                                          |
| **Usefulness** | Cannot directly support decision-making.                    | Essential for decision-making, predictions, and planning.                         |

>[!note]
Data  --->  ( Processing / Transformation )  --->  Information  --->  Knowledge

---
## 2. Life Cycle of Data
Data undergoes a structured life cycle inside an information system:
1. **Data Creation / Capture**: Collecting and inputting raw data (e.g., sensor logging, form submission).
2. **Data Management / Storage**: Organizing, storing, maintaining, and processing data efficiently.
3. **Removal of Obsolete Data**: Archiving or securely destroying outdated/redundant data to free resources and maintain accuracy.

---
## 3. Characteristics of Valuable Information
For information to be valuable to decision-makers, it must possess:
- **Timeliness**: Information must be available when needed before decisions are made.
- **Accuracy**: Must be error-free and accurate.
- **Contextual Relevance**: Presented in a clear, relevant context.
- **Enhanced Understandability**: Presented clearly (e.g., using charts, tables) so users grasp it easily.
- **Less Uncertainty**: Reduces ambiguity and doubt in decision-making.

---
## 4. Big Data & The 5 V's
**Big Data** refers to extremely large, complex datasets that traditional data processing application software cannot manage efficiently.

> [!KEY-CONCEPT] The 5 V's of Big Data
> 1. **Volume**: Massive scale of data generated (Terabytes to Petabytes).
> 2. **Velocity**: High speed at which new data is generated and processed in real-time.
> 3. **Variety**: Different data formats (Structured, Semi-structured, Unstructured like video, audio, text).
> 4. **Veracity**: Quality, trustworthiness, and accuracy of the data.
> 5. **Value**: The actionable insight and business value extracted from data analytics.

---
## 5. Drawbacks of Manual Methods vs. Emergence of ICT
### Drawbacks of Manual Data Processing:
- **Inconsistency & Data Duplication (Redundancy)**.
- **High Error Rate**: Susceptible to human miscalculations and omissions.
- **Processing Delays**: Time-consuming manual labor.
- **Lack of Information Sharing**: Data locked in physical paper files.
- **Infeasibility in Hazardous Environments**: Dangerous for humans to collect manual metrics in radioactive, toxic, or extreme environments.
### Advantages of ICT Era:
- Fast processing speed and automated execution.
- High accuracy and storage capacity.
- Real-time global communication over networks and WWW.

---
## 6. Applications of ICT in Various Domains

- **Education**: E-learning, Virtual Learning Environments (VLE), Learning Management Systems (LMS).
- **Healthcare**: Telemedicine, Electronic Health Records (EHR), Computer-Aided Diagnostics (CT/MRI).
- **Agriculture**: Precision farming, Automated irrigation, Weather forecasting apps, Drones.
- **Governance (E-Governance)**: G2C (Government to Citizen), G2B (Government to Business), G2G (Government to Government).
- **Commerce**: Online banking, E-Commerce platforms, Digital payment gateways.
